Hydraulic boundary &
Which mathematical pressure boundary inventory should the model state? &
Use \(\pl=p_\square=1.5\,\mathrm{MPa}\) on
\(\Gamma_\square=\Gamma_A\cup\Gamma_B\cup\Gamma_C\cup\Gamma_D\), and
\(\pl=p_E(t)\) on the niche boundary \(\Gamma_E\). &
Reference inventory.\\
Hydraulic boundary &
Which pressure Dirichlet conditions are attached in the checked run-local XML? &
The checked \path{02_process_variables_TRM.xml} attaches pressure Dirichlet data to
\path{cd-a_top} through \path{bc_top_pressure} and to \path{cd-a_niche4} through
\path{open_niche_seasonal}.  It does not explicitly attach left/right/bottom
pressure boundaries in that file. &
Implementation consistency gate.\\
Hydraulic boundary &
Are the outer-square pressure boundaries time-dependent? &
No.  The square-boundary pressure is the constant value \(p_\square=1.5\,\mathrm{MPa}\).
The only time-dependent hydraulic boundary in the current pressure inventory is the
open-niche curve \(p_E(t)\). &
Established.\\
Hydraulic boundary &
What does the negative niche-pressure curve mean? &
The values are OGS liquid-pressure values in the Richards sign convention.  Because
\(\pc=-\pl\), negative \(\pl\) represents positive suction/capillary pressure, not a
separate pressure variable. &
Established convention.\\
Hydraulic boundary &
What is known about the active niche curve \(p_E(t)\)? &
\path{open_niche_seasonal} is a \texttt{CurveScaled} parameter with unit scaling
factor.  The curve has 845 tabulated values, spans \(0\) to
\(1.3482\times10^8\,\mathrm{s}\), and ranges from about \(-5.23\) to
\(-53.24\,\mathrm{MPa}\). &
Numerically known.\\
Hydraulic boundary &
What remains unknown about the active niche curve \(p_E(t)\)? &
The current repository evidence does not close the source sensors, workbook columns,
filtering, smoothing, interpolation, time origin, and uncertainty for the active
curve. &
Boundary-input provenance gate.\\
Mechanical boundary &
Which displacement constraints are active on the square boundary? &
The model fixes \(u_x=0\) on \(\Gamma_A\cup\Gamma_B\) through
\path{cd-a_left}/\path{cd-a_right}, and fixes \(u_y=0\) on
\(\Gamma_C\cup\Gamma_D\) through \path{cd-a_top}/\path{cd-a_bottom}. &
Established.\\
Mechanical boundary &
What mechanical condition is used on the niche boundary? &
No displacement or traction condition is prescribed on \(\Gamma_E\) in the active
boundary-condition block.  The niche is therefore natural mechanically in the
current formulation. &
Established.\\
Mechanical boundary &
Is the top mechanical load active? &
No.  The \path{load_top} parameter is defined in the parameter file, but the
Neumann top-load boundary block is commented out in the process-variable XML. &
Inactive XML entry.\\
Thermal boundary &
Which temperature boundary and initial value are active? &
\(\TT=298.15\,\mathrm{K}\) is prescribed on \path{bulk_all}, and
\(\TT(\xx,0)=298.15\,\mathrm{K}\).  This constrains temperature to be homogeneous
and constant in the active model, although the TRM heat equation remains present in
the project. &
Established fixed context.\\
Initial state &
What is the active initial pressure field? &
\path{ic_pressure} is a function, but its vertical term is multiplied by zero.
The active initial liquid pressure is spatially uniform
\(1.5\,\mathrm{MPa}\). &
Established.\\
Initial state &
Does the initial pressure encode post-excavation drainage near the niche? &
No.  The current input has no heterogeneous initial drainage field from RH/suction,
direct pressure, nearby boreholes, or excavation history. &
Initial-state gate.\\
Initial state &
Does the model simulate staged excavation or gradual unloading? &
No.  The computation starts from the already-open 2D geometry, with
\(\uu(\xx,0)=\bm{0}\), no active initial stress tensor, and no active top load. &
Established limitation.\\
Initial state &
How should early pressure or displacement transients be interpreted? &
They may mix physical drainage with adjustment from the prescribed numerical initial
state.  Hard mechanical validation needs a prestress and excavation-reference
decision first. &
Interpretation gate.\\
OGS inputs &
Which OGS process and primary variables define the active model? &
The active process is \texttt{THERMO\_RICHARDS\_MECHANICS} with primary variables
\path{displacement}, \path{pressure}, and \path{temperature}.  The finite-element
orders are 2, 1, and 1, respectively. &
Established.\\
OGS inputs &
Which governing terms are explicitly inactive? &
The current formulation has zero specific body force, no explicit vapor-transport
phase terms, no active hydraulic source term, no active initial stress, and no
active top load. &
Established limitation.\\
OGS inputs &
Which parameter family is released in the first workflow? &
Only the intrinsic-permeability tensor field supplied through \path{k_i_rd} is
released as the first material field.  The current release audit keeps porosity,
retention, mechanics, thermal parameters, boundaries, and initialization fixed or
gated. &
Release policy.\\
OGS inputs &
How are permeability and porosity passed to OGS? &
The run-local project uses \texttt{MeshElement} fields: \path{k_i_rd} for
intrinsic permeability and \path{n_rd} for porosity.  The audited \path{n_rd} field
is spatially constant at \(0.105\). &
Established input mapping.\\
OGS inputs &
Are retention and relative permeability fitted now? &
No.  Saturation uses the fixed van Genuchten law with \(S_{lr}=0.1\),
\(S_{gr}=0\), \(m=0.45\), and \(p_b=10\,\mathrm{MPa}\).  Relative permeability uses
the matching fixed van Genuchten/Mualem-type law with minimum \(10^{-25}\). &
Fixed constitutive context.\\
OGS inputs &
Are mechanical parameters fitted now? &
No.  Orthotropic elasticity, \(\alpha_B=1\), Bishop exponent 1, and
saturation-dependent swelling are active but fixed.  Elastic-parameter release
requires hydro-mechanical residuals plus prestress/excavation policy. &
Gated.\\
OGS inputs &
Are thermal or liquid properties fitted now? &
No.  Density, viscosity, heat capacities, thermal conductivities, and thermal
expansivity are fixed XML inputs.  The \path{CTE}=1254.74 value remains a provenance
caveat rather than a calibrated thermal-expansion value. &
Fixed with caveat.\\
OGS inputs &
Should any material parameter be time-dependent in this first pass? &
No.  Time-dependent \(\KK(\xx,t)\), porosity, retention, fracture closure, and
healing/sealing inputs are excluded until repeated same-support evidence separates
material evolution from boundary forcing, saturation state, and spatial
heterogeneity. &
Deferred.\\
Model outputs &
Which VTU arrays are requested in the run-local workflow? &
The required run-local output arrays are \path{pressure}, \path{saturation},
\path{temperature}, \path{displacement}, and \path{porosity}. &
Established.\\
Model outputs &
Which process secondary variables are available but not required outputs here? &
\path{velocity}, \path{sigma}, \path{epsilon}, and \path{swelling_stress} are
process secondary variables.  They can be requested or sampled only with an
explicit output and interpretation policy. &
Available, not current required outputs.\\
Model outputs &
Is permeability an OGS state output? &
No.  \(\KK\) is an input mesh field, not a dynamic state variable.  Permeability
comparisons must sample or average the mesh field with the correct support and
direction operator. &
Input field.\\
Model outputs &
How should OGS water content be formed from outputs? &
The model-side mobile water-content proxy is
\(\theta=\por\Sl\), using porosity and liquid saturation.  This is not automatically
the same as total measured water content. &
Post-processing operator.\\
Model outputs &
How are output times tied to observations or boundary curves? &
The run starts at \(t=0\), identified by comments as 18 September 2019, and ends at
\(1.4\times10^8\,\mathrm{s}\).  Measurements require an explicit date-to-model-time
conversion and interpolation or matching rule. &
Time-mapping gate.\\
Model outputs &
When does an OGS field become a residual? &
Only after an observation operator defines the measured quantity, support, time
mapping, units, reference convention, and uncertainty.  Otherwise the OGS field is
only a diagnostic or model state. &
Residual-definition gate.\\
Material field &
What does intrinsic permeability describe materially? &
\(\KK(\xx)\) describes effective Darcy mobility of the clay/EDZ continuum before
relative permeability and viscosity are applied.  It is the base hydraulic material
field in the current inversion stage. &
Released base field.\\
Material field &
How does hydraulic conductivity relate to permeability? &
Hydraulic conductivity is derived from intrinsic permeability, relative
permeability, liquid density, viscosity, saturation state, and the chosen
head/gravity convention.  It is not an independent base field in the current OGS
input. &
Derived quantity.\\
Material field &
What does porosity describe and how does OGS use it? &
\(\por(\xx)\) is the effective pore-volume fraction used in storage, thermal mixing,
and \(\theta=\por\Sl\).  It is represented by \path{n_rd}, but the current field is
constant and not released. &
Fixed support field.\\
Material field &
What do saturation and retention describe? &
\(\Sl\) is the liquid occupancy of the OGS pore space.  It is computed from
\(\pc=-\pl\) through the fixed retention law, and it controls storage,
relative-permeability mobility, swelling, and water-content post-processing. &
Derived state plus fixed law.\\
Material field &
What does relative permeability describe? &
\(k_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sl)\) reduces liquid mobility under unsaturated conditions.  It
multiplies \(\KK/\mu_l\) in the Darcy velocity and is fixed by the current material
law. &
Fixed derived law.\\
Material field &
Is storativity a separate released field? &
No.  Storage behaviour follows from porosity, saturation derivative, liquid
compressibility/density, and mechanical volumetric coupling.  A separate
storativity field would be a new modelling choice. &
Derived/gated.\\
Material field &
How do Biot and Bishop fields enter the model? &
The fixed Biot coefficient is \(\alpha_B=1\), and the Bishop power law with exponent
1 gives \(b(\Sl)=\Sl\).  Together they scale the pore-pressure term in the
mechanical balance. &
Fixed coupling law.\\
Material field &
What is the elastic field description? &
The active mechanical model uses fixed orthotropic linear elasticity with
\(E=(13.8,6.0,13.8)\,\mathrm{GPa}\), \(G=(4.79,2.46,4.79)\,\mathrm{GPa}\), and
\(\nu=(0.44,0.22,0.44)\). &
Fixed constitutive field.\\
Material field &
Does the same bedding angle control mechanics, permeability, and electrical
anisotropy? &
The repository evidence supports bedding orientation as important context for
permeability candidates, but it does not prove that Darcy, mechanical, and
electrical anisotropy must share one hard angle in this chapter. &
Structural-prior gate.\\
Material field &
What role do swelling fields have? &
The model includes a fixed saturation-dependent swelling law with \(1\,\mathrm{MPa}\)
swelling pressures in each direction and \(0.1\leq \Sl\leq1\) limits.  It is active
physics but not a released parameter family. &
Fixed active law.\\
Material field &
How should faults, cracks, and EDZ features be classified here? &
They are not active discrete OGS geometry or separate material classes in this
chapter.  They can become structural priors, masks, equivalent fields, or scenarios
only after their source geometry, scale, aperture meaning, and 3D-to-2D projection
are defined. &
External field gate.\\
