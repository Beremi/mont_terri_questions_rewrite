\qaSubtable{Hydraulic Boundary Conditions}
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{0.18\textwidth} L{0.76\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\qaPair{qaEstablished}{Established}
{Which mathematical pressure boundary inventory should the model state?}
{Use \(\pl=p_\square=1.5\,\mathrm{MPa}\) on
\(\Gamma_\square=\Gamma_A\cup\Gamma_B\cup\Gamma_C\cup\Gamma_D\), and
\(\pl=p_E(t)\) on the niche boundary \(\Gamma_E\).}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{Which pressure Dirichlet conditions are attached in the checked OGS XML?}
{The checked \path{02_process_variables_TRM.xml} attaches pressure Dirichlet data
to \path{cd-a_top} through \path{bc_top_pressure} and to \path{cd-a_niche4}
through \path{open_niche_seasonal}.  It does not explicitly attach
left/right/bottom pressure boundaries in that file, so the implementation remains
a consistency gate.}
\qaPair{qaEstablished}{Established}
{Are the outer-square pressure boundaries time-dependent?}
{No.  The square-boundary pressure is the constant value
\(p_\square=1.5\,\mathrm{MPa}\).  The only time-dependent hydraulic boundary in
the current pressure inventory is the open-niche curve \(p_E(t)\).}
\qaPair{qaEstablished}{Established}
{What does the negative niche-pressure curve mean?}
{The values are OGS liquid-pressure values in the Richards sign convention.
Because \(\pc=-\pl\), negative \(\pl\) represents positive suction/capillary
pressure, not a separate pressure variable.}
\qaPair{qaChecked}{Checked input}
{What is known about the active niche curve \(p_E(t)\)?}
{\path{open_niche_seasonal} is a \texttt{CurveScaled} parameter with unit scaling
factor.  The curve has 845 tabulated values, spans \(0\) to
\(1.3482\times10^8\,\mathrm{s}\), and ranges from about \(-5.23\) to
\(-53.24\,\mathrm{MPa}\).}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{What remains unknown about the active niche curve \(p_E(t)\)?}
{The checked OGS project gives the active curve values, but the cited source
boundary does not close the source sensors, source columns, filtering, smoothing,
interpolation, time origin, and uncertainty for the active curve.}
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\qaSubtable{Initial State And Excavation Interpretation}
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{0.18\textwidth} L{0.76\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\qaPair{qaEstablished}{Established}
{What is the active initial pressure field?}
{\path{ic_pressure} is a function, but its vertical term is multiplied by zero.
The active initial liquid pressure is spatially uniform \(1.5\,\mathrm{MPa}\).}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{Does the initial pressure encode post-excavation drainage near the niche?}
{No.  The current input has no heterogeneous initial drainage field from
RH/suction, direct pressure, nearby boreholes, or excavation history.}
\qaPair{qaCaveat}{Caveat}
{Does the model simulate staged excavation or gradual unloading?}
{No.  The computation starts from the already-open 2D geometry, with
\(\uu(\xx,0)=\bm{0}\), no active initial stress tensor, and no active top load.}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{What measured in-situ stress is findable before excavation?}
{Cited CD-A papers give pre-excavation stress evidence, but not an active initial
condition for the current run.  The water-content modelling paper reports a measured
in-situ stress state projected to the mesh with compressive total stresses
\(\sigma_h=3\,\mathrm{MPa}\) and \(\sigma_v=7\,\mathrm{MPa}\)
\citep[Sec.~3]{WaterContentEDZ2024}.  The GETE modelling paper lists
\(\sig_0=-(5,5,7)\times10^6\,\mathrm{Pa}\) and notes that in-situ stress and pore
pressure measurements are challenging and change with excavation
\citep[modelling Table~2]{Ziefle2022GETE}.  The current active XML does not apply
either value: \path{ic_sigma0} and \path{load_top} are defined but inactive, so use
requires a component/sign convention, a principal-direction or rotation-angle
decision, and an excavation-reference decision.}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{Is a prestress angle or principal-stress orientation closed by the cited sources or XML?}
{No.  The cited papers give stress magnitudes and geological or structural
orientation context, but not an accepted principal-stress rotation for the current
2D prestress tensor.  CD-A bedding and niche-orientation evidence from the
characterization source cannot be substituted for stress orientation without a
modelling decision \citep[Sec.~4.3]{Ziefle2024Characterization}.  A usable
prestress definition would need principal directions or a rotation angle, component
order, sign convention, value choice, and the excavation-reference state; the
current active XML has no active initial-stress tensor.}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{How should early pressure or displacement transients be interpreted?}
{They may mix physical drainage with adjustment from the prescribed numerical
initial state.  Hard mechanical validation needs a prestress and
excavation-reference decision first.}
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\qaSubtable{OGS Inputs}
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{0.18\textwidth} L{0.76\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\qaPair{qaEstablished}{Established}
{Which OGS process and primary variables define the active model?}
{The active process is \texttt{THERMO\_RICHARDS\_MECHANICS} with primary variables
\path{displacement}, \path{pressure}, and \path{temperature}.  The finite-element
orders are 2, 1, and 1, respectively.}
\qaPair{qaEstablished}{Established}
{Which source hierarchy defines the equations and active implementation?}
{The active implementation is the transferred OGS project file and its included XML
files.  The equation notation follows the OGS TRM documentation and the
Wang--Kosakowski--Kolditz TRM notation used in the report.  If notation and checked
XML differ, the XML defines the active CD-A run and the mismatch is recorded as a
gate.}
\qaPair{qaChecked}{Checked input}
{Which numerical time-loop and solver settings are active?}
{The current run uses Backward Euler, fixed time stepping from \(0\) to
\(1.4\times10^8\,\mathrm{s}\), \texttt{basic\_newton} with maximum 150 iterations,
component-wise \(\Delta x\) convergence tolerances \(10^{-10}\) absolute and
\(10^{-8}\) relative, and the \texttt{PardisoLU} linear solver.}
\qaPair{qaCaveat}{Caveat}
{Which governing terms are explicitly inactive?}
{The current formulation has zero specific body force, no explicit vapor-transport
phase terms, no active hydraulic source term, no active initial stress, and no
active top load.}
\qaPair{qaPolicy}{Release policy}
{Which parameter family is released in the first workflow?}
{Only the intrinsic-permeability tensor field supplied through \path{k_i_rd} is
released as the first material field.  The current modelling policy keeps porosity,
retention, mechanics, thermal parameters, boundaries, and initialization fixed or
gated.}
\qaPair{qaPolicy}{Release policy}
{How do candidate-run parameter changes enter OGS?}
{They enter through current-run mesh fields or explicit XML/input replacements, such
as \path{k_i_rd} in the candidate mesh.  They are not silent edits to the governing
equations or to the exchanged source-model provenance.}
\qaPair{qaEstablished}{Established}
{How are permeability and porosity passed to OGS?}
{The current OGS project uses \texttt{MeshElement} fields: \path{k_i_rd} for
intrinsic permeability and \path{n_rd} for porosity.  The current \path{n_rd}
field is treated as fixed at \(0.105\).}
\qaPair{qaFixed}{Fixed context}
{Are retention and relative permeability fitted now?}
{No.  Saturation uses the fixed van Genuchten law with \(S_{lr}=0.1\),
\(S_{gr}=0\), \(m=0.45\), and \(p_b=10\,\mathrm{MPa}\).  Relative permeability
uses the matching fixed van Genuchten/Mualem-type law with minimum \(10^{-25}\).}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{Are mechanical parameters fitted now?}
{No.  Orthotropic elasticity, \(\alpha_B=1\), Bishop exponent 1, and
saturation-dependent swelling are active but fixed.  Elastic-parameter release
requires hydro-mechanical residuals plus prestress/excavation policy.}
\qaPair{qaCaveat}{Caveat}
{Are thermal or liquid properties fitted now?}
{No.  Density, viscosity, heat capacities, thermal conductivities, and thermal
expansivity are fixed XML inputs.  The \path{CTE}=1254.74 value remains a
provenance caveat rather than a calibrated thermal-expansion value.}
\qaPair{qaPolicy}{Release policy}
{Should any material parameter be time-dependent in this first pass?}
{No.  Time-dependent \(\KK(\xx,t)\), porosity, retention, fracture closure, and
healing/sealing inputs are excluded until repeated same-support evidence separates
material evolution from boundary forcing, saturation state, and spatial
heterogeneity.}
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\qaSubtable{Model Outputs And Observation Operators}
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{0.18\textwidth} L{0.76\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\qaPair{qaEstablished}{Established}
{Which VTU arrays are requested in the current OGS workflow?}
{The required output arrays are \path{pressure}, \path{saturation},
\path{temperature}, \path{displacement}, and \path{porosity}.}
\qaPair{qaChecked}{Checked input}
{Which process secondary variables are available but not required outputs here?}
{\path{velocity}, \path{sigma}, \path{epsilon}, and \path{swelling_stress} are
process secondary variables.  They can be requested or sampled only with an
explicit output and interpretation policy.}
\qaPair{qaEstablished}{Established}
{Is permeability an OGS state output?}
{No.  \(\KK\) is an input mesh field, not a dynamic state variable.  Permeability
comparisons must sample or average the mesh field with the correct support and
direction operator.}
\qaPair{qaPolicy}{Release policy}
{How should OGS water content be formed from outputs?}
{The model-side mobile water-content proxy is \(\theta=\por\Sl\), using porosity
and liquid saturation.  This is not automatically the same as total measured water
content.}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{How are output times tied to observations or boundary curves?}
{The run starts at \(t=0\), identified by comments as 18 September 2019, and ends
at \(1.4\times10^8\,\mathrm{s}\).  Measurements require an explicit
date-to-model-time conversion and interpolation or matching rule.}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{When does an OGS field become a residual?}
{Only after an observation operator defines the measured quantity, support, time
mapping, units, reference convention, and uncertainty.  Otherwise the OGS field is
only a diagnostic or model state.}
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\qaSubtable{Material And Constitutive Fields}
\begin{longtable}{@{}L{0.18\textwidth} L{0.76\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\qaPair{qaPolicy}{Release policy}
{What does intrinsic permeability describe materially?}
{\(\KK(\xx)\) describes effective Darcy mobility of the clay/EDZ continuum before
relative permeability and viscosity are applied.  It is the base hydraulic
material field in the current inversion stage.}
\qaPair{qaPolicy}{Release policy}
{How does hydraulic conductivity relate to permeability?}
{Hydraulic conductivity is derived from intrinsic permeability, relative
permeability, liquid density, viscosity, saturation state, and the chosen
head/gravity convention.  It is not an independent base field in the current OGS
input.}
\qaPair{qaFixed}{Fixed context}
{What does porosity describe and how does OGS use it?}
{\(\por(\xx)\) is the effective pore-volume fraction used in storage, thermal
mixing, and \(\theta=\por\Sl\).  It is represented by \path{n_rd}, but the current
field is constant and not released.}
\qaPair{qaFixed}{Fixed context}
{What do saturation and retention describe?}
{\(\Sl\) is the liquid occupancy of the OGS pore space.  It is computed from
\(\pc=-\pl\) through the fixed retention law, and it controls storage,
relative-permeability mobility, swelling, and water-content post-processing.}
\qaPair{qaFixed}{Fixed context}
{What does relative permeability describe?}
{\(k_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sl)\) reduces liquid mobility under unsaturated conditions.
It multiplies \(\KK/\mu_l\) in the Darcy velocity and is fixed by the current
material law.}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{Is storativity a separate released field?}
{No.  Storage behaviour follows from porosity, saturation derivative, liquid
compressibility/density, and mechanical volumetric coupling.  A separate
storativity field would be a new modelling choice.}
\qaPair{qaFixed}{Fixed context}
{How do Biot and Bishop fields enter the model?}
{The fixed Biot coefficient is \(\alpha_B=1\), and the Bishop power law with
exponent 1 gives \(b(\Sl)=\Sl\).  Together they scale the pore-pressure term in the
mechanical balance.}
\qaPair{qaFixed}{Fixed context}
{What is the elastic field description?}
{The active mechanical model uses fixed orthotropic linear elasticity with
\(E=(13.8,6.0,13.8)\,\mathrm{GPa}\), \(G=(4.79,2.46,4.79)\,\mathrm{GPa}\), and
\(\nu=(0.44,0.22,0.44)\).}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{Does the same bedding angle control mechanics, permeability, and electrical anisotropy?}
{The cited structural evidence supports bedding orientation as important context for
permeability candidates, but it does not prove that Darcy, mechanical, and electrical
anisotropy must share one hard angle in this chapter.}
\qaPair{qaFixed}{Fixed context}
{What role do swelling fields have?}
{The model includes a fixed saturation-dependent swelling law with
\(1\,\mathrm{MPa}\) swelling pressures in each direction and
\(0.1\leq \Sl\leq1\) limits.  It is active physics but not a released parameter
family.}
\qaPair{qaGate}{Gate}
{How should faults, cracks, and EDZ features be classified here?}
{They are not active discrete OGS geometry or separate material classes in this
chapter.  They can become structural priors, masks, equivalent fields, or
scenarios only after their source geometry, scale, aperture meaning, and
3D-to-2D projection are defined.}
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
